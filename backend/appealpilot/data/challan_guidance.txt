E-CHALLAN GUIDANCE (SUMMARIES, NOT STATUTE TEXT)
These are plain-language summaries drawn from the public sources listed in the README. They explain the process and are never quoted as law in a letter.

2.1 What an e-challan notice should contain. Photo evidence showing the offence and the vehicle's number plate; the device's measurement, such as the recorded speed; the date, time and place of the offence; the provision of the Act that was violated; and a certificate describing how the electronic record was produced. The notice goes to the registered owner by SMS, email or in physical form.

2.2 Records must be kept. Electronic records collected through monitoring must be kept until the challan is disposed of, so you can ask for the photo or video behind your challan.

2.3 Where to dispute a wrong e-challan. File a grievance on the official eChallan portal: enter the challan details, pick the issue that fits, explain why it is wrong, upload documents, and save the ticket number. If you suspect someone is using a duplicate of your registration number, also complain to the police.

2.4 Virtual court route. If the challan has moved to the Virtual Courts portal, you can contest it there. Contesting only begins the route to a regular court; it does not cancel the challan, and the online payment route no longer applies to that case.

2.5 Filing does not pause payment. Filing a grievance does not necessarily pause a payment deadline. Use the date printed on your notice, and check the portal for the rule at your stage.

2.6 Evidence that helps. Your registration certificate (RC); photographs showing your vehicle differs from the one in the challan image; dashcam or other video; FASTag records, parking receipts or service records showing the vehicle was elsewhere.

2.7 Signage and devices. State governments must notify the public about cameras and similar devices, which are meant to be placed with warning signs at high-risk stretches and critical junctions in larger cities.
